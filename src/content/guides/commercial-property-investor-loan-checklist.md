# Commercial Property Investor Loan Checklist

A commercial property investor loan checklist is a practical file-preparation tool for investors buying, refinancing, or releasing equity against income-producing commercial property. It helps you gather the lease, valuation, borrower, security, cash-flow, and settlement evidence lenders commonly need before they can assess a commercial investment property loan.

For Australian commercial property investors, the strongest finance applications usually answer three questions early: what is the property worth, how reliable is the income, and how will the borrower manage the debt if rent, valuation, or settlement timing changes. This checklist is designed to make those answers clear before the file reaches a credit team.

## Who This Is For

This checklist is for investors buying or refinancing commercial property such as warehouses, offices, retail shops, medical suites, mixed-use assets, or leased industrial units. It also suits business owners who hold a property as an investment rather than occupying it themselves.

If the property will be used by your own business, also read [owner-occupier commercial loans](/resources/guides/owner-occupier-commercial-loans-buy-your-business-premises). Owner-occupier files lean more heavily on trading cash flow, while investor files usually lean more heavily on lease quality, tenant strength, and property income.

## When To Use This Checklist

Use this checklist before sending documents to a lender or broker. A complete file can reduce back-and-forth, expose weak points early, and help match the transaction to the right funding path.

It is especially useful when you are working toward a contract deadline, refinancing a maturing facility, releasing equity for another project, or comparing a bank pathway with a faster specialist lender. If timing is very compressed, compare this guide with [fast commercial property loans](/resources/guides/fast-commercial-property-loans-for-urgent-settlement-in-australia) and [bridging finance](/resources/guides/bridging-finance-australia-complete-property-guide).

## When Not To Use This Checklist Alone

Do not rely on a checklist alone where the transaction has legal, tax, valuation, tenancy, or structural complexity. A checklist helps organise evidence, but it does not replace advice from a solicitor, accountant, valuer, or appropriately licensed adviser.

You should pause for professional input if the asset has a short WALE, vacant space, disputed rent, related-party tenancy, environmental concerns, planning restrictions, unapproved works, or a valuation gap. For valuation-sensitive deals, read [commercial property valuation dispute finance options](/resources/guides/commercial-property-valuation-dispute-finance-options-australia) before assuming the purchase price will equal lender value.

## The Core Commercial Property Investor Loan Checklist

A lender-ready file usually starts with the property. Prepare the contract of sale if purchasing, current title search, council rates, zoning details, building area, site area, outgoings, rent roll, lease documents, insurance details, and any building or pest reports already available.

For an existing holding or refinance, add the current loan statement, payout estimate, repayment history, latest valuation if available, and a simple explanation of what is changing. If you are releasing equity, state the purpose clearly rather than presenting the transaction as a vague cash-out request.

The income pack should include executed leases, option terms, expiry dates, annual rent, outgoings recovery, rent review mechanism, arrears position, incentives, bank guarantees, and any side agreements with tenants. Lenders usually prefer clean, documented rent rather than optimistic verbal summaries.

Borrower documents normally include company extracts, trust deeds if relevant, identification for directors, financial statements, tax returns, BAS, management accounts, asset and liability statements, and details of related entities. If the borrower is a trust, make sure the trustee and appointor details are consistent across documents.

## Property and Security Checks

The property needs to be understandable as security. Lenders will assess location, property type, condition, use, marketability, tenant profile, lease length, valuation, and likely resale demand if the loan ever needs to be recovered.

Create a one-page property summary covering address, asset type, land size, building size, current use, tenant, lease expiry, annual rent, outgoings, purchase price or estimated value, requested loan amount, and target settlement date. This simple summary helps a credit team quickly decide whether the file belongs with a bank, non-bank, private lender, or bridging lender.

If the asset has specialised use, explain the alternate use market. A standard industrial warehouse with multiple potential tenants is usually easier to assess than a highly specialised property with limited buyer demand. For more context, use [industrial property finance](/resources/guides/industrial-property-finance-warehouses-manufacturing) or [hospitality property finance](/resources/guides/hospitality-property-finance-restaurants-hotels-venues) where relevant.

## Lease and Tenant Evidence

For investment property lending, rent quality matters as much as borrower strength. A strong lease pack shows who pays rent, how much they pay, how long the lease runs, whether rent is current, and what happens at expiry.

Prepare the signed lease, disclosure statements if applicable, rent schedule, arrears ledger, tenant trading history if available, make-good obligations, option dates, and guarantees. If the tenant is related to the borrower, say so upfront because lenders may treat related-party rent differently from arm's-length rent.

Short lease expiry is not always fatal, but it changes the conversation. A property with six months left on the lease may need a re-tenanting plan, lower leverage, stronger borrower support, or a clearer refinance pathway. See [commercial property finance for short WALE assets](/resources/guides/commercial-property-finance-short-wale-assets-australia) if lease length is the main issue.

## Borrower and Serviceability Pack

Commercial property investors should prepare both property-income evidence and borrower-income evidence. Lenders may use rental income, business income, director income, other property income, or a combination depending on structure and policy.

A clear serviceability pack includes tax returns, financial statements, management accounts, lease income, existing loan commitments, credit limits, director income, and personal or entity liabilities. If there are one-off losses, abnormal expenses, or recent improvements, explain them in a short note rather than leaving the lender to guess.

For a deeper explanation of debt testing, read [commercial property loan serviceability](/resources/guides/commercial-property-loan-serviceability-how-much-can-you-borrow). The practical aim is simple: show that the property and borrower can carry the loan without relying on best-case assumptions.

## Deposit, Equity, and Loan-to-Value Evidence

Your checklist should show exactly how the equity contribution will be funded. That may include cash savings, retained profits, sale proceeds, refinance proceeds, partner contribution, or equity release from another property.

Commercial property deposits are often larger than residential-style deposits because lender appetite depends on property type, lease quality, borrower strength, and marketability. If a transaction depends on high leverage, prepare a backup plan for a lower valuation or lower approved loan amount.

If you are relying on equity from another property, include the title, existing loan statement, estimated value, and ownership structure for that security. Cross-collateralisation can help some files, but it also connects risks across properties, so compare options carefully with [cross-collateralised commercial property refinance](/resources/guides/cross-collateralised-commercial-property-refinance-australia).

## Settlement and Timing Pack

A finance file should make the timeline obvious. Include contract dates, finance clause expiry, settlement date, deposit paid, special conditions, valuation access details, solicitor contact details, and any critical third-party deadlines.

Timing determines lender choice. If the file has six to eight weeks and clean documents, a mainstream lender may be possible. If settlement is close, the borrower may need to compare non-bank lending, [private lending](/resources/guides/what-is-private-lending-australia), or a short-term bridge while longer-term finance is arranged.

Do not hide timing pressure. A lender can only price and structure around urgency if the deadline is known early. Last-minute surprises usually reduce options rather than improve negotiating power.

## Quick Self-Check Before You Apply

Before submitting, ask five practical questions. Can someone unfamiliar with the deal understand the property in two minutes? Is the lease income documented? Are the borrower entities clear? Is the requested loan amount supported by value and equity? Is there a realistic fallback if valuation or timing changes?

If any answer is weak, fix the evidence before shopping the file widely. Multiple incomplete submissions can create lender fatigue, especially where the same borrower is presented with changing numbers or missing explanations.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a commercial property investor loan checklist?

A commercial property investor loan checklist is a document list and preparation framework for investment property finance. It normally covers property details, leases, rent evidence, borrower documents, existing debt, deposit source, valuation support, and settlement timing.

### What documents do lenders usually need for commercial investment property finance?

Lenders commonly request a contract or title, leases, rent schedule, borrower financials, entity documents, tax returns, existing loan statements, asset and liability details, valuation access information, and settlement dates. The exact list depends on lender type, property type, loan amount, and borrower structure.

### Why do leases matter so much for commercial property investors?

Leases matter because they show the income supporting the property. Lenders review rent amount, expiry, options, arrears, tenant quality, incentives, outgoings, and whether the tenant is related to the borrower.

### Can I get finance if the commercial property has a short lease?

A short lease can still be funded in some cases, but it usually requires stronger equity, a credible re-leasing plan, lower leverage, or a specialist lender. The weaker the lease profile, the more important borrower strength and exit planning become.

### Should I apply before I have every document ready?

You can discuss the scenario early, but a formal lender assessment is stronger when key documents are ready. At minimum, prepare the property summary, lease evidence, borrower structure, requested loan amount, timing, and known issues before seeking terms.

No. This checklist is general information for commercial property investors and business borrowers. It does not consider your objectives, financial position, tax position, or legal obligations.
