# Mezzanine Finance in Australia: Capital-Stack Decision Guide

> **Direct answer:** Mezzanine finance is a junior layer of capital behind senior debt and ahead of ordinary equity. It may combine cash interest, capitalised amounts, fees, covenants and equity-linked returns. It fits only when the whole capital stack, senior-lender consent, cash flow and exit can support the junior layer under a downside case.

This page owns the mezzanine decision. For project senior debt and drawdowns, use the [property development loans guide](/resources/guides/property-development-loans-complete-funding-guide). For the complete sources-and-uses view, see the [commercial property capital-stack example](/resources/guides/capital-stack-commercial-property-finance-case-example).

## Where mezzanine sits

| Layer | Typical position | Primary return | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Senior debt | First-ranking or otherwise senior security | Interest and fees | Asset, cash flow and covenant failure |
| Mezzanine or junior debt | Behind senior debt under agreed priority | Cash interest, capitalised return, fees and sometimes equity features | Limited recovery after senior claims |
| Preferred equity | Contractual rights ahead of ordinary equity | Preferred return or profit share | No lender-style repayment certainty |
| Ordinary equity | Last-ranking ownership capital | Residual profit and value | First loss and full business risk |

Labels are not enough. The documents determine whether an instrument is debt, equity or a hybrid and how it ranks.

## When mezzanine finance may fit

- an acquisition has supportable senior debt but a defined funding gap;
- a development is fully costed and needs a documented junior layer behind senior construction debt;
- shareholders want to compare dilution with a more expensive junior facility;
- a recapitalisation has stable cash flow and a credible deleveraging plan; or
- timing between a value-creating milestone and long-term capital is measurable.

It is generally a poor fit when the business cannot pay senior obligations, the senior lender has not approved the structure, the project is not fully funded to completion, or repayment depends on an untested future valuation.

## Sources and uses must reconcile

Before discussing pricing, create a table covering every dollar.

### Sources

- borrower or sponsor equity;
- senior debt;
- mezzanine or junior debt;
- vendor or related-party funding; and
- grants, presale deposits or other eligible sources where applicable.

### Uses

- acquisition or land cost;
- construction and professional costs;
- taxes and statutory charges;
- finance interest and fees;
- working capital;
- contingency; and
- retained liquidity after completion or settlement.

If sources equal only the base budget and exclude finance cost, contingency or timing gaps, the transaction is not fully funded.

## What a mezzanine provider assesses

| Credit question | Evidence |
|---|---|
| What value sits below the facility? | Valuation, enterprise-value analysis or project feasibility |
| What ranks ahead? | Senior term sheet, facility, security and payout information |
| Is consent available? | Senior-lender approval and draft priority or intercreditor terms |
| Can cash cost be serviced? | Forecast cash flow and downside sensitivity |
| How does capitalised cost affect repayment? | Month-by-month debt schedule |
| What triggers default or control rights? | Covenant and enforcement summary reviewed by advisers |
| What is the exit? | Refinance, sale, cash flow or equity event with supporting evidence |

The provider will also assess management capability, related-party transactions, governance and the ability to report during the term.

## Senior consent and intercreditor terms

The senior lender may restrict additional debt, security, distributions or changes in control. A mezzanine proposal must be tested against those covenants before the borrower treats it as available capital.

An intercreditor or priority agreement can address payment blocks, notices, cure rights, standstill periods, enforcement control and distribution of proceeds. These terms can affect the junior lender’s economics and the borrower’s flexibility just as much as the headline price. Each party needs independent legal advice.

## Compare total economic cost

Request one schedule showing:

- cash interest and payment dates;
- interest or return capitalised into the balance;
- establishment, due-diligence, monitoring and legal fees;
- minimum return or early-exit provisions;
- extension and default economics;
- warrants, options, profit share or conversion rights;
- dilution under different enterprise values; and
- the total repayment on the expected and delayed exit dates.

An instrument with a lower cash coupon can be more expensive if capitalised amounts compound or equity participation becomes valuable. Conversely, equity can be more dilutive than a junior facility when the exit is clear and the business performs strongly. Model both rather than relying on generic market ranges.

## Project and development controls

For property development, the junior facility must be integrated with:

- verified cost to complete;
- quantity-surveyor reporting;
- senior draw conditions;
- contingency and cost-overrun responsibility;
- presale or leasing conditions;
- completion certification; and
- sales or refinance waterfall.

Junior capital cannot safely repair an unfunded base budget. The [commercial property development service](/services/commercial-property-development) should be considered with the entire project stack, not as a late add-on.

## Acquisition and business-growth controls

For an acquisition or growth transaction, lenders will test sustainable earnings, working capital after settlement, integration costs, customer concentration, management retention and debt service across the whole group. The funding pack should reconcile the purchase agreement, completion accounts, senior facility, shareholder contribution and post-transaction forecast.

If the transaction depends on rapid growth to make scheduled payments, run a downside case with delayed revenue and higher costs.

## Illustrative example

A sponsor funds a commercial development with equity and senior construction debt. A verified cost increase creates a gap before completion. Mezzanine capital may be considered only after the revised feasibility, cost-to-complete report, senior consent, priority terms and sale or refinance exit are documented.

This is hypothetical, not a client result. If the remaining value or exit cannot cover the senior and junior balances under a downside case, adding another layer would increase rather than solve risk.

## Application pack

- entity and ownership chart;
- sources-and-uses table;
- senior term sheet and security summary;
- valuation, feasibility or sustainable-earnings analysis;
- historical and forecast financial information;
- cash-interest and capitalised-balance schedule;
- draft intercreditor or priority principles;
- covenant and reporting plan;
- expected exit evidence; and
- downside and fallback analysis.

## Alternatives

Compare additional sponsor equity, vendor finance, a reduced transaction size, senior debt restructure, asset sale, joint venture, preferred equity or delayed completion. The right comparison depends on control, cash cost, dilution, security, certainty and the downside—not just the amount available.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is mezzanine finance always secured by property?

No. It can be supported by property, business assets, guarantees, contractual rights or equity-linked features. The actual documents control.

### Does mezzanine finance require senior-lender consent?

Often, but it depends on the senior documents and proposed structure. Confirm this before relying on the junior facility.

### Is capitalised interest free cash flow?

No. It postpones cash payment while increasing the balance due at exit.

### Is mezzanine cheaper than equity?

Not automatically. Compare total repayment, equity features, dilution and downside outcomes under realistic scenarios.
