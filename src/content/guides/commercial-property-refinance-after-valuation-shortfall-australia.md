A commercial property refinance valuation shortfall happens when the lender's valuation is lower than the borrower expected, reducing available leverage or causing the refinance to fall short of the payout amount. The borrower may still have options, but the next step depends on why the valuation moved, how much equity remains, and whether the exit or income story is still credible.

In Australia, this issue often appears when a business owner, investor, or developer is trying to refinance before maturity, release equity, clear arrears, or move from a short-term facility into a cleaner structure. The key is not to argue with the number emotionally. The useful response is to understand the valuation basis, provide better evidence where possible, and test realistic lender appetite before the refinance deadline becomes a default event.

## What Is a Commercial Property Valuation Shortfall?

A commercial property valuation shortfall is the gap between the value a borrower expected and the value accepted by the lender for credit assessment. Because most lenders size facilities against loan-to-value ratio, a lower valuation usually means a lower maximum loan amount.

For example, a borrower may expect a refinance facility to clear an existing commercial mortgage and provide extra working capital. If the lender valuation comes in lower, the proposed facility may only partly cover the payout, leaving a funding gap.

The shortfall can be frustrating, but it is not always final. Some files can be improved with stronger rental evidence, corrected property data, updated leases, clearer outgoings, or a different lender that has appetite for the asset type.

## Why Valuations Come In Lower Than Expected

### Lease quality and income risk

Commercial property values are heavily influenced by income. If leases are short, tenants are weak, rent is above market, incentives are unclear, or arrears exist, the valuer may adopt a more conservative view.

Borrowers with short leases should also read [refinancing commercial property with a short lease remaining](/resources/guides/refinancing-commercial-property-with-a-short-lease-remaining) and [commercial property finance for short-WALE assets](/resources/guides/commercial-property-finance-short-wale-assets-australia).

### Comparable sales and capitalisation rates

A borrower may focus on replacement cost or past purchase price, while the valuer focuses on recent comparable sales and the capitalisation rate for similar assets. If market yields have softened or buyers demand more return, the valuation can move down.

This is common in specialised, regional, vacant, or transitional properties where there are fewer clean sales to compare.

### Property condition and use

Deferred maintenance, environmental concerns, zoning limitations, fit-out dependence, access issues, or specialised plant can all affect marketability. A property that works well for the current owner may still be harder for a lender to treat as broadly saleable security.

### Refinance timing pressure

When a loan is close to maturity, borrowers sometimes discover the valuation issue late. That narrows the time available to gather evidence, negotiate with the existing lender, or test alternatives.

This is why refinance planning should start well before expiry where possible. The [commercial loan maturity refinance guide](/resources/guides/commercial-loan-maturity-refinance-australia) explains the early-warning process.

## When To Use a Refinance Strategy

A refinance strategy may be appropriate where the asset still has enough supportable value, the borrower can explain the valuation gap, and the replacement facility solves the actual pressure point. This may involve a lower loan amount, additional equity, added security, or staged funding.

It may also fit where the valuation shortfall is temporary. For example, a lease renewal may be close, a vacant tenancy may be under offer, or repair works may be near completion. In those cases, a short-term structure may bridge to a cleaner refinance later.

Borrowers should be realistic. If every lender returns a similar view, the market may be signalling that the borrower expectation is too high.

## When Not To Force the Refinance

Do not force a refinance if the new debt relies on an unsupported valuation, speculative future income, or a sale price that has not been tested. That can turn a valuation problem into a repayment problem.

A short-term private facility may also be unsuitable if there is no clear exit. Private credit can be useful for speed and flexibility, but it is not a substitute for a property that cannot carry the debt.

If the asset is genuinely overleveraged, selling, injecting equity, renegotiating with the existing lender, or reducing debt may be safer than adding another urgent facility.

## Practical Options After a Valuation Shortfall

### 1. Review the valuation assumptions

Ask what evidence the valuation relied on. Check land area, building area, lease details, rent, incentives, outgoings, tenancy status, zoning, and comparable sales. Basic factual errors can sometimes be corrected.

This is not about pressuring a valuer. It is about making sure the lender and valuer have complete, accurate information.

### 2. Strengthen the income evidence

Provide signed leases, rental statements, arrears position, outgoings reconciliation, tenant trading context where relevant, and evidence of market demand. For multi-tenanted assets, a clean tenancy schedule can make the file easier to assess.

If the issue is vacancy or lease expiry, the [re-tenant then refinance guide](/resources/guides/re-tenant-then-refinance-commercial-property-finance) may be a useful next step.

### 3. Test a different lender appetite

Different lenders can treat the same asset differently. A bank may be conservative around short leases or non-standard income, while a private lender may focus more heavily on equity, exit, and marketability.

That does not mean a private lender will ignore valuation risk. It means the structure, term, and conditions may be different. The [commercial property loan after a bank decline guide](/resources/guides/commercial-property-loan-after-bank-decline-non-bank-options-australia) covers this pathway.

### 4. Reduce the requested loan amount

If the refinance only fails because the requested equity release is too high, reducing the loan size may be cleaner than chasing a more aggressive lender. This is especially relevant where the main goal is to clear an existing facility rather than extract cash.

### 5. Add security or restructure the facility

Some borrowers can add another commercial property, use a lower LVR, split facilities, or combine property security with business cash-flow evidence. Cross-collateralisation has risks and should be reviewed carefully, but it can sometimes solve a narrow shortfall.

For related structure issues, read [cross-collateralised commercial property refinance](/resources/guides/cross-collateralised-commercial-property-refinance-australia).

### 6. Use short-term bridging with a defined exit

Bridging finance may fit where the valuation issue is transitional and there is a credible event ahead, such as lease renewal, sale, refinance, or settlement. It should be sized around a realistic exit, not optimism.

If there is a hard deadline, [fast commercial property loans](/resources/guides/fast-commercial-property-loans-for-urgent-settlement-in-australia) may also be relevant, but speed should not replace due diligence.

## Document Checklist

- Existing loan payout figure and maturity date.
- Current valuation report or lender valuation summary where available.
- Lease documents, tenancy schedule, rental statements, and arrears notes.
- Rates notices, outgoings, insurance, and property expenses.
- Recent sales evidence or agent appraisals, clearly labelled as market evidence, not formal valuation.
- Building condition, environmental, or maintenance reports if relevant.
- Borrower financials, bank statements, and conduct history.
- Written refinance purpose and use of funds.
- Exit strategy if the proposed facility is short term.

## Example Scenario

A business owner expects an industrial property to value high enough to refinance an expiring bank facility and release extra working capital. The lender valuation comes in lower because the major tenant's lease has only nine months remaining and recent comparable sales show softer yields.

The borrower has three choices. They can reduce the cash-out request and refinance only the existing debt, negotiate a lease extension before retesting the file, or consider a short-term private facility while the lease issue is resolved.

The right answer depends on the maturity deadline, tenant strength, equity position, and repayment plan. A valuation shortfall is a signal to tighten the file, not a reason to accept the first expensive offer.

## FAQs

### What happens if a commercial property valuation is too low for refinance?

A low valuation can reduce the maximum loan amount and create a refinance shortfall. The borrower may need to provide better evidence, reduce the loan request, add security, test another lender, bridge temporarily, or consider sale or equity injection.

### Can I dispute a commercial property valuation?

A borrower can usually provide factual corrections or additional market evidence, but the lender controls whether a valuation review is accepted. A dispute is strongest when it identifies specific errors or missing information, not just disagreement with the result.

### Will a private lender use a higher valuation?

A private lender may assess the file differently, but it will still care about security value, marketability, leverage, and exit. Private credit can be more flexible than a bank, but it does not make valuation risk disappear.

### Can bridging finance solve a valuation shortfall?

Bridging finance can help when the shortfall is temporary and there is a defined exit, such as lease renewal, sale, refinance, or incoming settlement funds. It is risky when used without a credible repayment event.

### What evidence helps after a valuation shortfall?

Helpful evidence can include signed leases, tenancy schedules, rental statements, recent comparable sales, updated outgoings, maintenance records, environmental reports, and a clear refinance purpose. The goal is to make the lender's risk assessment easier.

### Should I refinance before my commercial loan matures?

Yes, borrowers should usually start well before maturity because valuation, lease, lender, and documentation issues can take time to resolve. Waiting until expiry reduces options and increases pressure.
