A notice to complete is a formal notice a party can serve after the contract completion date passes. It usually sets a new deadline and can make time essential. If you receive one, speak to your solicitor straight away and work out on the same day whether your existing lender can still settle or you need a short-term alternative.

This page is general information for a buyer purchasing commercial property or business premises for a business purpose. It does not replace advice from your solicitor. Emet Capital is a commercial finance broker, not a lender. It does not lend, fund, approve or settle loans.

## What a notice to complete means for a buyer

In plain terms, a notice to complete is used when the agreed completion date has passed and a party still wants the other party to complete. On the [Law Society of NSW and REINSW 2026 standard contract](https://www.lawsociety.com.au/sites/default/files/2026-03/LS4831_PAP_ContractLand2026Contract_Watermarked_2026-02-24.pdf), clause 15 says the parties must complete by the date for completion and, if they do not, a party can serve a notice to complete if that party is otherwise entitled to do so. The signed contract controls. Other states and territories use their own contract forms.

The period in the notice is not a single figure you can read off a website. It depends on the contract, any special conditions and the applicable law. Your solicitor confirms the deadline and the form of the notice. This guide does not interpret the notice.

The [Law Society of NSW page on finalising a sale](https://www.lawsociety.com.au/for-the-public/know-your-rights/selling-a-home/finalising-sale) records that late settlement can involve interest and, in limited cases, termination of the sale. That is general selling-a-home information. Your solicitor confirms what your contract provides.

NSW Government buying-property guidance says settlement [usually takes place around 6 weeks after contracts are exchanged](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/buying-and-selling-property/buying-property-nsw/contracts-and-deposits), and that NSW settlements are completed electronically. Commercial contracts often name a different date. Use the date in your contract, not a typical residential timetable.

## First 24 hours: what to check

Work through this list with your solicitor and, where finance is still open, with the existing lender or broker the same day:

- The deadline your solicitor confirms from the notice and the contract.
- Whether the existing approval can still settle: valuation status, outstanding conditions and missing documents.
- The exact funds-to-complete figure your solicitor lists.
- Whether the vendor will consider a settlement extension. That discussion is solicitor-led. The contract may provide for costs if completion is late.
- What security is available: the property being purchased, other business-use property, existing debt and who owns each title.

Keep a written list of what is known, what is assumed and what is still missing. A settlement delayed by the bank, a late valuation or an incomplete condition list is a document problem as well as a calendar problem.

## Finance options if the original lender cannot settle in time

If the original lender cannot meet the solicitor-confirmed date, the next step is a comparison, not a product label. [business.gov.au](https://business.gov.au/finance/funding/apply-for-a-business-loan) notes that documentation requirements vary between loans and that it is a good idea to compare loan products from multiple lenders before applying. None of the options below is a recommendation, a quote or an approval.

| Option | When it may fit | Main trade-off | What lenders usually ask for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Push the existing lender | Outstanding conditions, documents or a valuation are the only remaining gaps | The original credit decision may still change if conditions are not met | A clear condition list, the people responsible for each item, and evidence that each item is in progress |
| Negotiate an extension | The vendor may agree, through solicitors, to a later completion date | The contract may provide for interest or other costs; agreement is not automatic | The reason for the delay, the new date sought, and who pays any contract costs |
| Short-term [bridging finance](/services/bridging-finance) secured by property | The purchase is still proceeding and a later refinance, sale or other repayment event can be explained | A short-term facility is assessed on security and exit, not on speed claims | Contract and notice, funds to complete, title and debt, valuation, business purpose and exit |
| A [second mortgage](/services/first-second-mortgages) over other business-use property | Another property has usable equity and can support a second ranking security | Priority, existing-lender consent and the first-mortgage position all need to be established | Both titles, existing facility details, purpose, repayment plan and the funds-to-complete statement |
| A [private first-mortgage term loan](/services/private-lending) | A non-bank first mortgage over business-use property may replace or stand in for the original facility | The lender still assesses the property, purpose and repayment; it is not an automatic fallback | The same lender-ready pack, with a term and exit that match the stated purpose |

Caveat loans are not an option on this page. Emet Capital does not arrange consumer or household loans. Residential property used as security can still be discussed only where the borrowing purpose is a genuine business purpose.

If funds are short rather than late, the existing [settlement shortfall finance guide](/resources/guides/settlement-shortfall-finance-in-australia-what-to-do-when-funds-are-short-before-settlement) covers that separate problem. A notice to complete can sit on top of a shortfall, but the two issues are not the same.

## What a lender needs to move quickly

Lenders do not fund from a phone call. A complete pack lets a credit team see the deadline, the security and the way the loan is repaid. Typical items include:

- The contract and any notice to complete, plus solicitor comments on the date that actually applies.
- A funds-to-complete statement that matches the contract price, deposits already paid, adjustments and remaining costs.
- Title and existing-debt details for each property offered as security, including owner names and any other registered interests.
- A valuation or valuation instructions the proposed lender will accept.
- Evidence that the funds are for a business purpose, such as occupation of premises, a business purchase sitting with the property, or another documented commercial use.
- An exit plan: how the short-term loan is to be repaid, and what still has to happen before that event.
- Identification and entity documents for the borrower, owners and any proposed guarantor.

NSW settlements are prepared in an electronic workspace and, when documents and funds are ready, lodged with [NSW Land Registry Services](https://www.nswlrs.com.au/). A lender-ready pack still has to match what the solicitor and the incoming mortgagee need for that lodgment. This list is not a promise that a lender will assess, approve or settle by any date.

business.gov.au describes a secured loan as a loan backed by collateral or security, such as property. If the loan is not repaid, the lender can take the security to cover losses. That exposure exists once a property is offered as security, including on a short-term facility used to complete a purchase.

## Exit plan: how a short-term loan is repaid

Lenders assess the exit first because a short-term loan is only useful if there is a realistic way to repay it. Common exits include refinance to the original lender once remaining conditions are met, refinance to another lender, sale of a property, or other documented business proceeds. For the product definition, see [what bridging finance is](/resources/guides/what-is-bridging-finance).

An exit is not an approval. A broker discussion, a valuation in progress or a future application does not bind a take-out lender. Say what is confirmed, what is in progress and what would delay the exit. If the only repayment idea is hope that a bank will later say yes, the file is not ready.

Open and closed bridging structures treat the exit differently. The [open versus closed bridging loan comparison](/resources/guides/open-vs-closed-bridging-loan-complete-comparison) explains that distinction. The right structure, if any, follows the facts of the purchase and the exit, not a preference for a product name.

## Illustrative scenario (not a client outcome)

This is a hypothetical scenario, not an Emet Capital client matter and not a predicted result.

A trading company exchanges on a warehouse it will occupy for its own operations. The original lender is still waiting on a valuation when the completion date in the contract passes. The vendor then serves a notice to complete. The directors call their solicitor the same day. The solicitor confirms the deadline in the notice and whether the vendor will consider an extension. In parallel, the broker lists outstanding lender conditions, the funds still required to complete, the title position and a refinance exit once the valuation and remaining conditions are done. No amount, rate or date in this example is a quote, a past deal or a typical outcome.

## When this is the wrong path

Short-term, property-secured finance is the wrong path if the business cannot show a credible exit. A valuable warehouse does not replace an explainable repayment event. It is also the wrong path if the purchase is for personal or residential use. Emet Capital only arranges business-purpose finance.

If your solicitor advises that the notice is invalid, or that a dispute about the contract is likely, stop treating the file as a straightforward funding race. Those are solicitor issues. A lender cannot repair a contract dispute by issuing an approval. If the only security is unsuitable, or owners will not support the borrowing, comparing extra product labels will not close the gap.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I get bridging finance after a notice to complete is served?

A served notice does not, by itself, stop a lender from assessing a business-purpose bridging request. The lender still needs the contract and notice, funds to complete, security and title details, valuation evidence, business purpose and a documented exit. Whether anything can be done before the solicitor-confirmed deadline depends on those facts. Speak to your solicitor about the contract and to a broker about whether a lender can consider the file.

### Does a notice to complete mean I lose my deposit?

That depends on the contract and the circumstances. This page does not say that a deposit will or will not be lost, recovered or applied in any particular way. Ask your solicitor what the notice and the contract provide, including any default and completion clauses. On the finance side, establish the same day whether the existing approval can still settle or whether a short-term, property-secured alternative should be compared.

### What do lenders need to fund before a notice to complete deadline?

They need a usable picture of the deadline, the money required, the security and the exit. That usually means the contract and notice, funds to complete, title and existing-debt details, valuation material, business-purpose evidence, identity and entity documents, and a repayment plan that is more than a hope of later refinance. No pack guarantees assessment, approval or settlement by the notice date. Missing items are delays, not paperwork trivia.

### Can a second mortgage fund a settlement shortfall on a commercial purchase?

It may be considered where another business-use property can support second ranking security and the purpose is a genuine business purpose. The lender still assesses both titles, existing debt, priority, purpose and the exit. A second mortgage does not fix an incomplete file. If the issue is a shortfall rather than a missed date, read the [settlement shortfall finance](/resources/guides/settlement-shortfall-finance-in-australia-what-to-do-when-funds-are-short-before-settlement) guide as well.

### What if the original lender is the reason settlement is delayed?

A settlement delayed by the bank is still a file the solicitor and the broker can unpack: outstanding conditions, valuation, documents and whether the approval remains live. Escalating that list is the first finance option. If the original lender cannot meet the confirmed date, the comparison in this guide (extension, bridging, second mortgage or a private first mortgage) is the next discussion. It is not a finding that the bank must complete, or that another lender will.
