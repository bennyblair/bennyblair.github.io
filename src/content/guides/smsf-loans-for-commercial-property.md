> **Direct answer:** An SMSF can borrow to buy a single acquirable asset only through a limited recourse borrowing arrangement. The asset is held in a holding trust. Lender rights against the SMSF trustee are limited to that asset. From 10 August 2026, a real-property LRBA entered into on or after that date to finance an asset acquired after that date must be for business real property. That limit does not apply to maintaining or refinancing a borrowing entered into before 10 August 2026, or to an acquisition that happens under an arrangement entered into before that date. ## How limited recourse borrowing works

The [Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Act 1993](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A04633/latest/text) generally prohibits a regulated superannuation fund from borrowing. Limited recourse borrowing arrangements in sections 67A and 67B are a narrow exception. The ATO's [about LRBAs](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-investing/restrictions-on-smsf-investments/smsf-borrowing-restrictions/limited-recourse-borrowing-arrangements/about-lrbas) page describes the structure as follows.

An LRBA is an arrangement where the:

- SMSF trustee obtains a loan that is used to purchase an acquirable asset
- asset is held in a separate trust from the SMSF, known as a holding trust
- SMSF acquires beneficial interest in the asset and after repaying the loan has the right to legal ownership of the asset
- other assets of the SMSF are protected if the loan defaults

While the asset is not held directly by the SMSF, any investment returns earned from the asset go to the SMSF. Superannuation law does not specify the type of trust that must be used as a holding trust, but the SMSF trustee must have a beneficial interest in the asset and the right to acquire legal ownership after making one or more payments. A discretionary trust cannot be used as the holding trust. The holding trust cannot be one in which the SMSF trustee is one of a number of unit holders.

Any investment must still meet the fund's investment strategy and governing rules. The SIS Act sole purpose test requires each trustee to ensure the fund is maintained solely for purposes that include providing retirement benefits to members. Present-day personal use of the asset is not allowed.

## Single acquirable asset, repairs and improvements

The ATO [rules for entering an LRBA](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-investing/restrictions-on-smsf-investments/smsf-borrowing-restrictions/limited-recourse-borrowing-arrangements/rules-for-entering-an-lrba) state that borrowed money must be used to acquire either a single asset, or a collection of identical assets that have the same market value and can be treated as a single asset.

The borrowed money can also be used to pay for expenses from the borrowing or asset acquisition, such as loan establishment costs or stamp duty, and for maintaining or repairing the asset. The borrowed money cannot be used to improve an asset. The asset cannot be subject to a charge other than as provided in relation to the borrowing by the SMSF.

Substantial renovations, development or a change that would make the asset a different asset are a separate compliance problem during the loan, not only a credit issue. Replacement of the asset is allowed only in limited circumstances set out in the SIS Act and ATO guidance.

## Business real property rules from 10 August 2026

Changes to the LRBA provisions for real property commenced on 10 August 2026. The [Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2026A00049/asmade/text), Schedule 5, amends SIS Act subsection 67A(2) so that for an asset that is real property, the asset is business real property. That amendment applies to arrangements entered into on or after 10 August 2026 under which money is borrowed, or borrowings of money are maintained, for the acquisition of assets. It does not apply to the extent that the arrangement is for maintaining or refinancing a borrowing of money under another arrangement entered into before that commencement, or the acquisition of the asset happens under an arrangement entered into before that commencement.

The same ATO rules page states that LRBAs entered into on or after 10 August 2026 to finance a real property asset acquired after this date, must only be for business real property. Those arrangements must be maintained or refinanced while still meeting the definition of business real property. The identity of the lender does not determine whether the real property must be business real property.

The ATO also states that the changes do not apply if an SMSF has exchanged a binding contract to acquire real property before 10 August 2026, even if the contract is settled or the LRBA is entered into after this date, or if the fund maintains or refinances an earlier LRBA on or after 10 August 2026.

The ATO [changes to limited recourse borrowing arrangements](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-investing/restrictions-on-smsf-investments/smsf-borrowing-restrictions/limited-recourse-borrowing-arrangements/changes-to-limited-recourse-borrowing-arrangements) page also states that an asset under an LRBA to which these changes apply must remain business real property for the life of the arrangement. A period when it is not is a borrowing breach that later resumption does not erase.

Business real property generally means land and buildings used wholly and exclusively in a business. It is an exception to the in-house asset and related party acquisition rules. Whether a particular site meets that test is a legal and factual question for the fund's advisers.

Residential property that is not business real property cannot be financed under a real-property LRBA to which those 10 August 2026 changes apply. An SMSF may still be able to hold some residential property without borrowing, if the rest of superannuation law is met.

## Related-party purchases and leases

The ATO's [SMSF investment restrictions](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-investing/restrictions-on-smsf-investments/what-are-the-smsf-investment-restrictions) page states that your SMSF cannot acquire an asset from a related party unless the price reflects the market value and the asset is in an allowed class. Allowed classes include listed securities, business real property, and limited in-house assets. Related parties include members, relatives, controlled companies and trusts, and standard employer-sponsors.

Business real property leased between the fund and a related party is an exception to the in-house asset rules. Any lease must be made on an arm's length basis and reflect the market value. If an asset is not acquired or sold at arm's length, all or part of any income from the transaction may be non-arm's length income and taxed at the highest marginal rate.

Using the SMSF as a guarantor for a member or a member's relative is not allowed. Related-party leases of business premises still attract the arm's-length, documentation and NALI rules.

## What lenders assess

There is no single deposit, loan-to-value ratio, interest rate or term that applies to every SMSF commercial property loan. Acceptable leverage, pricing, term, repayment type and covenants are set by each lender's policy and by the property, lease, fund liquidity and documents in front of them.

A lender will usually want to understand the SMSF deed, holding trust, limited-recourse security, property, lease, related occupancy, rent and other fund cash flow, and how the fund would meet payments if rent paused.

Understanding [first and second mortgages](/resources/guides/second-mortgages-for-business-guide) helps when comparing security ranking, but an SMSF LRBA is not a standard company mortgage. Under SIS Act section 67A, the rights of the lender against the SMSF trustee, and any right of a person to be indemnified by the trustee because of a personal guarantee given by that person in favour of the lender, are limited to rights relating to the acquirable asset. A personal guarantee is a separate right against the guarantor. Members should get legal advice before signing any guarantee.

The Australian Government's [business-loan guide](https://business.gov.au/finance/funding/apply-for-a-business-loan) notes that it is a good idea to compare loan products from multiple lenders before applying. Documentation requirements vary between loans.

## Documents that usually support an application

Typical items include the SMSF deed, investment strategy, financial statements, contract of sale, valuation, lease, holding-trust documents and a funds-to-complete statement.

Related-party occupancy needs a written lease and evidence that rent and terms match the market. Missing deed powers, an incomplete holding trust or an improvement plan funded from the loan are common reasons a file cannot be submitted as an LRBA.

For commercial property owned outside superannuation, [commercial property refinancing](/services/refinancing-solutions) is a different pathway. Do not assume an SMSF refinance can use the same structure as a company or family-trust loan.

## Tax and NALI caution

This guide does not state SMSF tax rates, CGT discounts or penalty percentages. Tax treatment depends on whether the fund is complying, whether income is ordinary or non-arm's length, and whether assets support a retirement-phase income stream. The ATO explains those categories in [How SMSFs are taxed](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-administration-and-reporting/how-smsfs-are-taxed).

Non-arm's length income is a live risk on related-party leases and purchases that are not at market value. NALI is taxed at the highest marginal rate.

Get written SMSF tax advice before exchanging contracts.

## Main risks

- the property is not a single acquirable asset, or borrowed money is used to improve it
- a real-property LRBA to which the 10 August 2026 changes apply is used for an asset that is not business real property
- the holding trust does not give the SMSF the required beneficial interest and purchase right
- a related-party lease or purchase is not at market terms, creating NALI or other breaches
- rent, vacancies or pension payments leave the fund unable to meet the loan
- trustees treat a broker discussion as approval, tax advice or superannuation advice

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can my SMSF borrow money to buy commercial property?

Yes, but only through a complying limited recourse borrowing arrangement for a single acquirable asset held in a holding trust. From 10 August 2026, an LRBA entered into on or after that date to finance real property acquired after that date must be for business real property, subject to the maintain, refinance and pre-commencement exceptions above. Lender rights on default against the SMSF trustee relate to that asset rather than the rest of the fund.

### What deposit or loan-to-value ratio applies to SMSF commercial property loans?

There is no single percentage that applies to every fund or property. Deposit, leverage, term and pricing are set by each lender's policy and by the asset, lease, liquidity and documents. Members should not assume that a figure quoted for ordinary commercial property finance will be available inside an SMSF.

### Can my business lease commercial property owned by my SMSF?

A related-party lease of business real property can be allowed if it is at arm's length and at market value, and if the in-house asset exceptions apply. Related-party terms that are not commercial can create non-arm's length income and other breaches. Document the lease properly and get SMSF legal and tax advice before occupying fund property.

### How is rental income from SMSF commercial property taxed?

It depends on whether the fund is complying, whether the income is ordinary or non-arm's length, and whether the asset supports a retirement-phase income stream. This page does not quote tax rates. Use current ATO SMSF tax guidance and advice from an SMSF accountant. Do not assume pension-phase income is automatically exempt.

### Can I improve or renovate commercial property owned by my SMSF while an LRBA is in place?

Borrowed money can be used to maintain or repair the asset. The borrowed money cannot be used to improve an asset. Major works that change the character of the asset can breach the single acquirable asset rules. Get advice before any renovation, not after invoices are issued.

### What happens if my SMSF cannot service the loan payments?

Limited recourse is intended to confine lender rights against the SMSF trustee to the acquirable asset, which can still mean sale of that property, losses in the fund and compliance issues. Other fund assets are the point of the limited-recourse design, not a promise that members or related businesses are unaffected. Conservative cash-flow planning is part of trustee duty, not a lender marketing feature.
