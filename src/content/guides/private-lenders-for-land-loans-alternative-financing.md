Private lenders assess land loans by looking at the land as security, the business purpose, how much equity the borrower will contribute, and a documented exit that can repay the facility. For a business borrower or investor buying vacant, rural or development land after a bank decline, that file is usually judged on those points rather than on a standard home-loan serviceability model.

This guide is general information on commercial, business-purpose land finance. It is not a rate sheet, an eligibility promise, or a guide to owner-occupier home loans or first-home-buyer land.

This page is for business-purpose borrowing only. [ASIC's information on disputes about commercial loans](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/contact-us/reporting-misconduct-to-asic/disputes-about-commercial-loans) is Information Sheet 207 (INFO 207). The laws, and the protections they provide to borrowers, differ depending on the purpose of the loan. The law provides the lowest level of protection to commercial loans, including loans to small businesses. Commercial loans are loans provided to companies and business owners. Lenders that only provide commercial loans are not required to have a credit licence and are not legally required to be a member of AFCA.

## Understanding Private Land Lending

Private lenders for land loans still take security over the land, but they weigh equity, planning status, location, borrower experience and a credible repayment event more heavily than a long trading history against rent. A file a bank will not take can still be presented to a private or non-bank lender. Presentation does not mean an offer.

### Why Banks Decline Land Finance

Banks often treat vacant land conservatively. Undeveloped land usually produces no rent while holding costs and planning work continue, valuation can move with planning news, and the next buyer pool can be thinner than for an income-producing building. If a subdivision, rezoning or development application is refused, delayed or loaded with conditions, the security story changes. Contamination, overlay or access issues can have the same effect.

That is not a rule that banks never fund land. Many business files still fail bank policy because of zoning, location, income, documents or timing. The [commercial property loans Australia complete guide](/resources/guides/commercial-property-loans-australia-complete-guide) explains how improved commercial assets are assessed.

### How Private Lenders Assess Risk

Two questions usually sit at the centre of a private land file: how much of the lender's money sits against the land if a sale is forced, and how the loan is repaid within the facility. Lenders usually require a meaningful equity contribution and set it case by case against land type, location, planning status and the rest of the borrower's position.

Exit evidence matters as much as the purchase contract. Common business-purpose exits include sale of the site, refinance into construction or development funding once approvals are in place, staged sale after subdivision, or refinance against an improved asset. Lenders often prefer borrowers who have completed a similar project. A first project is not automatically declined, but the planning evidence, budget and delivery team then have to work harder.

### Types of Private Land Lenders

The private land market is not one product. Individual private lenders, pooled private credit funds, specialist non-bank lenders and some family offices all appear, with different mandates. A broker's job is to match the land, the purpose and the exit to lenders that actually assess that type of file, not to rank a public lender list or quote a market rate.

## Eligibility and Assessment Criteria

Availability is always lender-specific. Prepare the file around equity, planning, documents and exit rather than asking whether a private land loan exists in general.

### Preferred Land Types

Lenders tend to prefer land with a use that other buyers could also understand. Residential development sites with demand, services and a planning pathway are usually easier to discuss than isolated lots. Commercial and industrial land can attract interest where zoning and access support the intended project. Rural land is assessed as agribusiness or development, not as a lifestyle block. Lifestyle or hobby-farm purchases sit outside this guide.

Planning status is a spectrum. Land with a current approval for the intended use is usually easier to underwrite than land that needs a new consent, and land that needs rezoning is harder again. Lenders still buy unapproved sites when the pathway is evidenced.

### Borrower Requirements

Private lenders look at the person or entity behind the land, not only the title. They typically want identification, a clear borrowing entity, an asset and liability position, and enough other resources to hold the land if the project runs long. Other debt, unpaid tax or litigation does not automatically end a file, but it has to be explained. Credit history is usually less decisive than in bank home lending, and it is not ignored. Recent defaults or unpaid judgments can still tighten conditions or stop a lender.

### Documentation Requirements

Documentation requirements vary between loans, but you may need to provide proof of identification, your business plan, financial reports, including cash flow statements (if available), financial forecasts, lease agreements and your personal financial information. On a land file, lenders also commonly want the contract or terms of sale, title and planning certificates, comparable sales or a valuer's instructions, a use or development summary, and evidence of the exit. Private lenders usually instruct a valuer who understands land, not only improved buildings. Compile those items into one pack.

## What Drives Cost

Private land loans usually cost more than a bank facility on an income-producing commercial building. The premium reflects vacant security, specialised assessment and a shorter intended life, not a published national rate. This page does not quote interest rates, fees or leverage ratios.

### Cost, Fees and Comparison

Cost follows risk: land type, location, planning status, equity contribution, term, borrower track record and how clean the documents are. Establishment, legal, valuation and brokerage charges may sit on top of interest. Some facilities charge interest in cash. Others allow interest to capitalise, which preserves cash and increases the amount that must be repaid at exit.

Compare the whole proposal, not a headline figure. [business.gov.au](https://business.gov.au/finance/funding/apply-for-a-business-loan) says to read each loan's terms and conditions carefully to compare upfront and ongoing charges, interest rates, term lengths, security and other conditions. Do not treat the first offer as the market, and do not rely on unsolicited approaches.

### How Long Facilities Last

How long a private land loan lasts depends on the lender, the exit and the project. These facilities are usually short relative to a bank commercial mortgage because they are meant to cover a purchase, a planning or holding period, and a documented repayment event. Interest-only structures are common so principal is repaid from the exit. Extension rights, if they exist, are a later credit decision and should be discussed when the facility is documented.

If the next step is a building programme, compare [commercial property development finance](/services/commercial-property-development) as a later path. A land facility and a construction facility are different assessments.

### Comparing Lender Options

Specialist land lenders and generalist private lenders will not price or condition the same site the same way. Reputation, documentation quality and whether the lender can actually complete matter as much as the term sheet. Using a commercial finance broker can help you compare mandates, but the borrower still decides. An approval is not promised.

## Structuring Deals for Approval

Structure will not turn a weak site into a strong one, but a clear pack can stop a workable file from looking like a vague enquiry.

### Maximising Equity Position

Lenders usually require a meaningful equity contribution and set it case by case. A larger cash contribution, a purchase below evidenced value, or extra security (for example another property offered in support) can lower the lender's exposure. Cross-collateralisation only helps when the extra asset is real, documented and acceptable to that lender.

### Demonstrating Exit Strategies

Write the exit as a sequence with evidence: a feasibility and planning pathway for a development site, survey and comparable lot evidence for a subdivision, or a refinance path that does not depend on a hoped-for value jump. A labelled hypothetical: two similar-sized lots in the same corridor can still be treated differently if one has a current approval and a contracted end use, and the other has only a concept sketch. That illustration is not a transaction and it does not predict leverage, price or approval.

### Professional Presentation

A short executive summary, the contract, planning documents, photos, a cost outline and the borrower's relevant experience should sit in one pack. Approaching a lender that does not take the land type wastes time. Discuss broker fees before work starts.

## Managing Private Land Loans

Keep the exit on track and the lender informed. Private land loans are poor long-term holding tools because the cost sits on land that is not yet earning.

### Cash Flow Management

Budget interest, rates, insurance and planning costs with a buffer if the approval or sale runs long. Running out of holding funds can trigger default. Capitalising interest preserves cash and grows the debt that the exit must clear, so the feasibility has to work on the higher balance.

### Extensions and Refinancing

Ask about extension mechanics when the loan is documented. A later emergency request, with no progress to show, is a weaker conversation. Start the next facility while the current one still has time on it. Waiting until maturity compresses every option.

### Lender Communication

Tell the lender early if planning, a contractor or a buyer delays the exit. Silence until the repayment date is worse than a revised timetable with evidence.

## Alternative Land Financing Strategies

A registered private mortgage is not the only way to complete a land purchase. This page does not decide which structure fits a live file.

### Vendor Finance Options

Some vendors will leave part of the price in as vendor finance, taking mortgage security and receiving instalments instead of full cash at settlement. Terms are negotiated. There is no standard rate, term or deposit. Each party should have their own solicitor review the contract. This page does not describe tax treatment of vendor finance.

### Joint Venture Partnerships

A capital partner can fund some or all of the purchase in exchange for ownership and a share of profit instead of interest. Splits are negotiated. They need a written agreement covering default, decisions and exit.

### Progressive Purchase Arrangements

Buying a first stage and holding an option over the balance can cut the initial funding need. Options need clear exercise windows, price mechanics and default provisions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much equity do private land lenders usually want?

Lenders usually require a meaningful equity contribution and set it case by case. The land type, location, planning status, other security and the borrower's overall position all affect how much cash or extra security is asked for. There is no single deposit percentage that applies to every vacant, rural or development site.

### What drives the cost of a private land loan?

Cost follows risk: the land, the location, planning status, equity, documents, term and the borrower. Interest, establishment, legal and valuation charges can all apply, and some facilities capitalise interest. This page does not quote rates or fees. Compare full terms across lenders rather than a headline figure.

### How long do private land loans last?

Duration is not a published market standard. It follows the lender's mandate, how long the planning or holding work needs, and when the documented sale, refinance or development funding can repay the facility. An extension, if it exists at all, is a later credit decision rather than an automatic extra term.

### Can I get a private land loan with impaired credit?

Sometimes. Private lenders often weigh land equity, purpose and exit more heavily than a bank credit score, and they may still decline. Recent defaults, unpaid judgments or an unexplained history can tighten conditions or stop the file. Older, explained issues with a rebuilt position are usually one factor among several, not a separate product.

### What is the difference between private land loans and bank land loans?

Banks often treat vacant land conservatively, with heavier documentation and a stronger focus on other income to service the loan. Private lenders may still consider land types, timing or structures that sit outside bank policy, with more weight on equity and exit. Private facilities are not automatically faster, larger or cheaper. Each lender decides.

### Do I need development approval before applying for a private land loan?

No. Private lenders commonly assess purchases before a development approval is issued, if the planning pathway is evidenced. A current approval for the intended use usually supports a more confident view. Land that needs rezoning or unusual concessions is harder. Lenders want a realistic path, not a hope that consent will appear.

## Conclusion

Private lenders assess land loans as a security, purpose and exit problem. Vacant, rural and development sites can be funded for a business purpose when the equity, planning story and repayment event are clear. They are a poor fit when the only plan is to hold land indefinitely.
