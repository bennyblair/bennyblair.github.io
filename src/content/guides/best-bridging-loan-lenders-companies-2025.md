Commercial bridging finance lenders should be compared against a defined transaction, not placed in a generic “best lender” ranking. A lender that suits a contracted metropolitan property sale may not accept an open refinance exit, specialised commercial security or a complex ownership structure.

## Types of commercial bridging lenders

| Lender category | Typical decision approach | Confirm before relying on an indication |
|---|---|---|
| Bank or established non-bank | Policy-led assessment with fuller serviceability and documentation | Whether the required term and settlement date fit policy |
| Specialist property lender | Defined property, location, leverage and exit rules | Valuation method, accepted security and open/closed exit appetite |
| Private credit fund | Credit-committee assessment within an investment mandate | Mandate, funding certainty, due diligence and decision timetable |
| Smaller private lender or syndicate | Case-by-case judgement and negotiated structure | Source of funds, lender of record and any third-party approval |
| Development or transitional lender | May assess construction, lease-up or repositioning risk | Milestones, quantity surveyor or project conditions and exit requirements |

Categories overlap. Ask who will provide the funds, who issues formal approval and which facts could cause the lender or capital source to change.

## Match the lender to the transaction

Begin with the current obligation and exit. Is the bridge funding a purchase, maturity, settlement shortfall or temporary transition? Is repayment from a contracted sale, an uncontracted sale, refinance or another liquidity event? The certainty, evidence and expected timing of the exit shape lender appetite.

Then map the security: property type, location, ownership, value evidence, leases, existing debt and title interests. Commercial property can require review of leases, vacancy, environmental or planning matters. A lender's headline leverage or speed statement is not meaningful until the actual property and priority are accepted.

Finally, disclose borrower and entity facts, including guarantees, credit conduct, arrears, litigation or insolvency issues. These do not always prevent assessment, but they can change conditions, pricing and timetable.

## Written lender comparison matrix

Use the same assumptions for every offer:

- borrower, guarantor and security entities;
- current obligation and required settlement date;
- property and valuation assumption;
- existing debt and proposed security priority;
- gross facility and cash available after deductions;
- interest rate, calculation, payment and capitalisation;
- establishment, valuation, legal, line, management and discharge costs;
- minimum-interest or early-exit provisions;
- term, extension mechanics and delayed-exit cost;
- approval and settlement conditions;
- reporting, control and guarantee requirements;
- default events, default pricing and enforcement expenses;
- accepted exit and evidence required.

Model the total repayment at the expected exit and at a delayed date. A facility with lower initial pricing may be less suitable if it has a rigid maturity, expensive extension or conditions that the transaction cannot meet.

## Indicative terms are not approval

An initial quote normally relies on unverified facts. It can change after valuation, title review, credit assessment, legal due diligence or new information. Ask the lender or broker to list every assumption and condition.

Conditional approval is also not the same as funds available at settlement. Conditions may include a satisfactory valuation, legal review, first-mortgagee consent, evidence of the exit, executed security documents, insurance or repayment of another debt. Formal approval can still be subject to completion steps.

Avoid committing to a purchase or telling another creditor that funds are certain based only on an informal indication. Obtain legal advice on transaction deadlines and finance conditions.

## What a bridging finance broker adds

A broker should test product fit, organise a consistent evidence pack, target lenders whose mandates align and compare written terms. The broker should explain why lenders were included or excluded and distinguish known amounts from estimates.

Ask the broker how many lenders received the file, whether each saw the same facts, which conflicts or fees apply, and what could still change. A large lender panel is not valuable if the scenario was sent to lenders that cannot accept the property or exit.

A broker cannot guarantee approval, valuation, legal completion or settlement timing. The strongest service is clarity about dependencies and trade-offs, not certainty that no party can responsibly provide.

## Red flags

Pause if the proposed lender is not clearly identified, bank details or legal representatives cannot be verified through trusted channels, fees are unexplained, terms are verbal, independent legal advice is discouraged, or the facility relies on an exit the lender has not examined.

Also investigate a quote that assumes a higher value, lower existing debt or shorter term than other offers. Apparent price differences often reflect different inputs. Ask for a corrected, like-for-like comparison.

ASIC notes that misleading conduct, unconscionable conduct and unfair contract terms may be relevant in commercial lending disputes. Commercial borrowers should still obtain independent legal review because consumer-credit rules do not necessarily apply.

## Lender selection sequence

1. Define the obligation, required net proceeds and settlement date.
2. Document property, title, existing debt and entity structure.
3. Evidence the primary exit and a delayed-exit contingency.
4. Shortlist lenders whose mandates fit those facts.
5. Obtain written indicative terms with assumptions and conditions.
6. Compare expected and delayed total cost.
7. Select on executable fit, downside and total terms—not rate or speed alone.
8. Complete independent legal and other professional review.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which lender offers the cheapest commercial bridge?

There is no reliable universal answer. Cost depends on property, priority, borrower, amount, term, interest treatment, fees and exit. Compare written terms for the same scenario and date.

### Are private bridging lenders faster than banks?

They may use different assessment processes, but timing still depends on the file, valuation, legal work and conditions. Private does not mean automatic or guaranteed.

### Should I apply directly or use a broker?

Either can work. Direct applicants must identify suitable lender mandates and compare terms themselves. A broker may add value by targeting, packaging and comparison, provided the process and fees are transparent.
