EV charging equipment finance is business-purpose funding used to buy or deploy commercial electric vehicle charging infrastructure. A project budget may include charger hardware, switchboard upgrades, cabling, protective equipment, installation, commissioning and site-control technology.

Do not assume every project cost will be treated as one financeable asset. Give the proposed lender an itemised budget and ask which hardware, installation, software and professional costs it will include. Finance assessment also remains separate from electrical, site and connection checks.

This guide is for Australian business owners, fleet operators, commercial property investors and developers planning workplace, depot, destination or public charging. For the broader funding pathway, see Emet Capital's [asset finance service](/services/asset-finance).

## Who This Guide Is For

This guide covers commercial charging projects at depots, workplaces, offices, retail centres, accommodation venues, car parks and developments.

A finance request is easier to explain when the business can document its right to use the site and identify who owns each part of the system. If the operator does not own the land, provide the lease, licence, landlord consent or host agreement and identify where it deals with installation, electricity supply, maintenance and removal.

## What EV Charging Project Costs May Be Financed?

Start with an itemised project budget that separates the equipment purchase from fixed works and ongoing expenses. This gives the proposed lender a clear list to assess instead of a single undifferentiated project total.

| Cost category | Examples | Question to resolve |
|---|---|---|
| Charger hardware | Charging units, pedestals and enclosures | Who buys and owns each unit? |
| Electrical infrastructure | Switchboards, transformers and metering | Is it a separate asset or a permanent building improvement? |
| Site works | Trenching, cabling, bollards and reinstatement | Will the proposed facility include installation costs? |
| Control technology | Load management, payment terminals and communications | Is it purchased hardware, licensed software or a subscription? |
| Professional costs | Design, engineering, certification and applications | Must these fees be paid from business cash? |
| Operating costs | Electricity, maintenance, insurance and network services | These are usually recurring expenses rather than acquired equipment. |

A turnkey supply-and-install invoice can simplify documentation, but the lender still needs to confirm what it will fund. Show the hardware value, installation cost, professional fees, recurring charges and any amount the business will pay directly.

## Confirm the Site and Electrical Scope Before Seeking Finance

A charger quote is not a complete project scope. Before committing to funding, the business should confirm the proposed load, existing electrical capacity, switchboard requirements, metering, protection, cabling and any upstream works. A qualified electrical provider should document what must happen before the equipment can be commissioned.

Australian Government guidance distinguishes charging levels and explains that charger power and vehicle characteristics affect charging speed. Its installation guidance also notes that a dedicated charger may require a licensed electrician, switchboard work or a supply-connection upgrade.

Keep charger hardware, electrical upgrades and connection-related costs as separate budget lines until the technical scope is confirmed. This makes cost changes visible and prevents the finance request from relying on an incomplete vendor estimate.

## Match Site Control to the Finance Term

The business should compare the proposed finance term with the documented period of site access. If the site arrangement can end earlier, record what happens to the chargers and any unpaid finance.

Depending on the property, evidence may include title, a lease, a licence, a host-site agreement, landlord consent or strata approval. Australian Government guidance notes that strata occupants may need permission from the managing strata body and that applicable laws vary by state or territory.

Document whether the charger can be removed, who owns cabling and switchboard work, and who pays reinstatement costs. These are contract questions for appropriate legal and property advisers, not assumptions to leave until installation.

## Compare Ownership and Operating Models

There is no single ownership model for commercial EV charging. The contract determines who owns the charger, pays for electricity, controls pricing, maintains the system and carries utilisation risk.

| Model | Typical commercial purpose | Main contract question |
|---|---|---|
| Fleet charging | Charge business vehicles at a depot | Does existing business cash flow support the project? |
| Workplace or property amenity | Provide charging for staff, tenants or visitors | Who bears installation and ongoing electricity costs? |
| Charging as a service | Obtain a managed charging solution | What is included, and what happens at termination? |
| Revenue-share host site | Allow an operator to install public chargers | Who owns the customer relationship, hardware and revenue? |
| Equipment purchase or lease | Acquire chargers for business use | Who owns the equipment during and after the term? |

Business.gov.au explains that a leasing company owns leased equipment, while buying gives the business ownership. EV projects can be more complex because the charger, installed electrical works and software rights may have different owners. Review all related contracts together rather than comparing only the equipment price.

## Build Conservative Revenue and Savings Assumptions

For public or destination charging, projected revenue is a forecast rather than completed trading history. The model should show sessions, energy delivered, customer pricing, electricity charges, transaction fees, maintenance, downtime and any revenue share. It should also test lower utilisation and delayed commissioning.

Fleet projects may create operational savings rather than direct sales. Explain the number of vehicles, travel pattern, charging window and alternative charging cost. Any claimed saving should include the infrastructure, electricity, maintenance and financing costs required to achieve it.

A repayment plan that depends entirely on an untested charging site's best-case utilisation is fragile. Show whether the established business can carry the facility during a slower ramp-up period.

## What Documents Help Explain the Project?

A clear finance request connects the borrower, equipment, site, delivery plan and repayment source. Prepare documents that let the proposed lender verify the request, including:

- business entity, ownership and financial information;
- itemised charger and electrical quotes;
- charger specifications, quantities, supplier details and warranties;
- title, lease, licence, host agreement or site consent;
- electrical assessment, designs and relevant connection correspondence;
- software, payment, monitoring and maintenance contracts;
- project timetable and milestone-payment schedule;
- fleet plan, host-site agreement or conservative revenue model; and
- a schedule showing which costs are requested under the facility and which will be paid from cash.

The document pack should use consistent names, figures and dates. If the supplier quote excludes electrical or network works, state that directly and show how the missing scope will be funded.

## A Practical Finance Sequence

1. Define the use case, site and required charging capacity.
2. Confirm the business's right to install and operate the equipment.
3. Obtain electrical and connection input appropriate to the site.
4. Separate hardware, installation, approvals, software and operating costs.
5. Confirm who owns each asset and contract right.
6. Build a conservative repayment case with downside scenarios.
7. Ask which project costs the proposed lender will consider.
8. Compare total cost, security, early-exit terms and end-of-term ownership.
9. Obtain appropriate electrical, legal, tax and financial advice before committing.

## When EV Charging Equipment Finance May Fit

Finance may be worth investigating when the project has a defined commercial purpose, itemised costs, documented site rights and a repayment source that does not rely on an aggressive forecast. Business.gov.au notes that businesses can consider a loan when they cannot pay for expensive equipment upfront, while buying and leasing create different ownership outcomes.

Pause the finance decision when the electrical scope is unresolved, site access may end before the proposed term, the business cannot cover costs outside the facility, or repayment depends on speculative utilisation. Resolve those project questions before adding debt.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can EV charger hardware and installation be financed together?

There is no universal treatment. Give the proposed lender a supply-and-install breakdown covering hardware, civil works, professional fees, connection charges and software, then obtain written confirmation of the costs included before contracts are signed.

### Can switchboard upgrades and cabling form part of the facility?

Ask the proposed lender to assess them as separate budget lines rather than assuming they are included with the charger hardware. Also document their cost, ownership and whether the business or property owner is responsible for the work.

### Can projected public charging revenue support an application?

Projected revenue can support a commercial business case, but it is not established income. Use traceable assumptions for utilisation, electricity cost, downtime, fees and site tenure, then show how the business manages a slower ramp-up.

### Who owns a commercially financed EV charger?

Ownership depends on the agreement. The business may own equipment subject to lender security, a lessor may retain ownership, or a charging operator may own hardware at a host site. Installed works and software rights may follow different arrangements.

### Does finance approval confirm that the charger can be connected?

No. Finance approval does not confirm electrical capacity, network access, technical compliance, landlord or strata consent, planning requirements or commissioning. Those checks remain separate from the credit process.
