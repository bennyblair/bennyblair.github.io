There is no single deposit figure for a commercial property loan in Australia. For a purchase, the deposit is the part of the purchase price the lender will not fund. [business.gov.au](https://business.gov.au/finance/funding/apply-for-a-business-loan) notes that loans vary a lot in the amount you can borrow, whether you need to provide security, and their costs and conditions. Lenders may ask for details of the property, any lease and your business finances. Because those terms vary, the cash needed to complete the same purchase can differ from one lender to another. That is a reason to compare written terms, not a rule that a particular property, lease or financial profile always changes the deposit by a set amount.

The deposit is also not the whole cash requirement. Budget for set-up costs and other upfront fees that the lender discloses, and for stamp duty where it applies. In [NSW](https://www.revenue.nsw.gov.au/taxes-duties-levies-royalties/transfer-duty) and [Queensland](https://qro.qld.gov.au/duties/transfer-duty/), the buyer generally pays transfer (stamp) duty on a purchase. [Victoria](https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/buying-property/land-transfer-stamp-duty) also charges land transfer duty. Victoria is [progressively replacing land transfer and landholder duty on commercial and industrial property](https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/commercial-and-industrial-property-tax) with an annual commercial and industrial property tax (CIPT). CIPT is payable 10 years after the property enters the reform. Whether a later dealing is exempt from duty depends on that transaction, including whether it is a tax reform scheme transaction and whether the property has a qualifying use; check the [State Revenue Office dealings page](https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/commercial-and-industrial-property-tax/dealings-properties-have-entered-reform). Ask the lender and your solicitor which other third-party costs apply to your file rather than assuming a standard extras list. Ask each lender, in writing, whether it funds a percentage of the purchase price or of the valuation it accepts, and what happens if those two numbers differ.

## What a Purchase Deposit Covers

A commercial property loan deposit covers the part of the purchase price the lender will not fund. If the lender is willing to fund a lower share of the price or of the value it accepts, the buyer has to contribute the difference.

That sounds simple, but commercial buyers often treat the deposit as the full cash requirement. It usually is not. Stamp duty, where it applies, and the set-up costs the lender discloses still have to be paid. Other third-party costs, such as legal work, a valuation or searches, may also need separate funding unless the written offer says the loan will cover them.

A more accurate framing is this: the deposit is the buyer’s equity contribution into the purchase price, while total funds to complete are that contribution plus transaction costs the loan will not cover.

## Planning formula: deposit, valuation and costs

Deposit and loan-to-value ratio are two sides of the same arithmetic. Read the formula below with [Commercial Property LVR Explained: Maximise Your Borrowing](/resources/guides/commercial-property-lvr-explained-maximise-your-borrowing). The ratio describes the lender’s share. The deposit and extra costs describe what you still have to fund.

Use this only as a planning formula. Replace every letter with figures from a written offer, or leave the line blank until you have one. It is not a typical commercial deposit, an Emet Capital product, or a prediction of approval.

**Assumptions.** The lender’s written offer funds X% of the lower of (1) the valuation it accepts and (2) the purchase price. Stamp duty, where it applies, and the lender’s disclosed set-up fees are not included in that advance. Any other third-party costs are funded by the buyer unless the offer says otherwise.

Then:

1. Lending basis = the lower of accepted valuation and purchase price.
2. Lender funds = X% of that lending basis.
3. Amount still needed toward the price = purchase price − lender funds.
4. If the accepted valuation is below the purchase price, that gap is a shortfall. It sits inside step 3; the lender does not fund it.
5. Total cash to complete = amount still needed toward the price + stamp duty where it applies + disclosed set-up and other third-party fees the loan will not cover.

If the lender uses a different basis, discard this formula and follow the written offer. X is not a market average. It is whatever that lender writes for that file.

## What lenders may ask to see

[business.gov.au](https://business.gov.au/finance/funding/apply-for-a-business-loan) lists financial reports, forecasts and lease agreements among documents you may need to provide. It also recommends comparing products from multiple lenders, including non-bank lenders, because costs and conditions vary.

Treat property type, occupancy, remaining lease term, tenant details and your financials as questions a lender may assess. Do not assume a mainstream warehouse, a long lease or a strong tenant will reduce the deposit, or that a vacant property, a short lease or a specialised asset will increase it, until that lender says so in writing.

A bank, non-bank and private lender can set different amounts, fees and conditions for the same file. Do not assume cheaper pricing means more leverage, or the reverse. Compare the amount offered, the security required, set-up costs and other conditions on the same written summary.

If the borrower’s financials, serviceability and overall position are part of the assessment, the companion guide on commercial property loan serviceability explains how income, leases, expenses and existing debt can feed a borrowing discussion. That still does not fix a deposit percentage in advance.

## How to work out total funds to complete

Build a one-page worksheet before you treat any deposit figure as complete:

1. Record the purchase price and any conditions in the contract.
2. Ask each lender, in writing, for the percentage it will fund and whether that percentage applies to the price, the valuation it accepts, or the lower of the two.
3. Apply the planning formula above with those figures.
4. Add stamp duty using the revenue office for the state of the property, and note Victoria’s CIPT transition if the asset is commercial or industrial there.
5. Add the set-up costs and ongoing fees disclosed in the offer, plus any third-party costs your solicitor lists.
6. Confirm timing: which amounts are due on exchange, which at settlement, and which can be paid from loan proceeds.
7. Repeat the worksheet for each written offer instead of copying one lender’s answer onto another.

The useful output is a cash-to-complete number for that offer, not a market rule of thumb.

## When the cash requirement can rise after you start

The cash you need can rise if a later written offer funds less of the price, if the accepted valuation is below the contract price, or if duty and fees were left out of the first estimate. It can also change if you move to a different lender because timing or documents do not fit the first option.

Treat vacancy, a short remaining lease, thin financials or a specialised asset as reasons to ask the lender how it will assess the file, not as rules that always increase the deposit.

## Can equity from another property help?

Potentially, yes. Some business borrowers use equity from another asset to support a purchase or the extra transaction costs. That may happen through refinance, extra security, or another property-backed structure.

This can reduce the need for fresh cash, but it also changes the risk profile because more assets may become tied into the transaction. The question to settle in writing is the available equity or security position, and what is being offered as security, not whether that extra property counts as a “deposit”.

## What to ask if the valuation differs from the price

If the lender’s accepted valuation is lower than the contract price, the planning formula above increases the amount you still need toward the price. That can surprise buyers who treated an early deposit estimate as locked in.

In practice, a lower accepted valuation can:

1. increase the cash you still need toward the price
2. reduce the lender’s maximum loan amount
3. lead you to renegotiate with the seller
4. lead you to compare a different lender’s written terms

None of those outcomes is automatic. Ask what the offer uses as its lending basis before you sign.

## Should you stretch to reduce the cash contributed?

Not always. Contributing less cash can preserve liquidity, but only if the debt remains serviceable and the written terms still fit. A buyer who maximises the loan without leaving cash for fitout, working capital, or trading stability can create a problem immediately after settlement.

Commercial property finance is strongest when the capital structure fits both the property and the operating business. If the purchase absorbs every dollar of available liquidity, the transaction may be technically fundable but commercially fragile.

## Common mistakes

### Treating deposit as the only number

The real requirement is total funds to complete, not just the equity slice into the purchase price.

### Copying one lender’s figures onto another

[business.gov.au](https://business.gov.au/finance/funding/apply-for-a-business-loan) says loans vary in amount, costs and conditions. Compare written offers on the same worksheet.

### Treating the contract price as the lending basis

A signed contract does not tell you whether the lender will fund a percentage of that price, of a valuation, or of the lower of the two.

### Leaving no liquidity after settlement

If the deal empties the business bank account, the structure may create operating pressure straight away.

### Confusing speed with suitability

A fast offer can solve a timing problem, but not every fast structure is the best longer-term answer.

## FAQs

### What is a commercial property loan deposit?

A commercial property loan deposit is the buyer’s equity contribution into a purchase before the lender funds the balance. It is usually only one part of the total cash required to complete. For a refinance, the equivalent question is the available equity or security position, not a deposit.

### Is the deposit the only money I need for a commercial property purchase?

No. Add stamp duty where it applies, set-up costs the lender discloses, and any other third-party fees your solicitor lists unless the written offer says the loan will cover them. Use the planning formula in this guide so those items are not left out.

### Why can one lender ask for more cash than another?

Because loans vary in the amount you can borrow, the security required and their costs and conditions. The same purchase can produce a different cash-to-complete figure once each lender’s written percentage, lending basis and fees are put on the same worksheet.

### What happens if the valuation is lower than the purchase price?

If the offer funds X% of the lower of accepted valuation or price, a lower valuation reduces the lender’s advance and increases the amount you still need toward the price. Confirm that basis in the written offer before you treat any estimate as final.

### Can I use equity from another property as part of the purchase funds?

Potentially, yes. Some borrowers use refinance proceeds, extra security, or another property-backed structure to support a purchase or broader transaction costs. The trade-off is that more assets may become tied into the transaction. Describe that support as available equity or security, not as a deposit.
