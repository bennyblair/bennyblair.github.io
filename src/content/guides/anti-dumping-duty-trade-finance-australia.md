# Anti-Dumping Duty and Trade Finance for Australian Importers

Anti-dumping duty trade finance in Australia is business funding structured around an importer's supplier, freight, customs, duty and customer-payment timeline when goods may attract an anti-dumping or countervailing measure. The finance does not determine whether a measure applies or calculate the legal duty liability. It addresses the commercial cash gap created when the landed cost must be paid before inventory is sold and customer receipts arrive.

Anti-dumping measures are additional duties imposed on particular goods from particular exporters or countries after Australia's trade-remedies process finds the relevant conditions are met. Countervailing measures respond to subsidised imports. For an importer, the practical issue is specific classification and current-measure verification: product description, tariff classification, country of origin or export, exporter, shipment date and applicable notice can all matter.

This guide explains the funding workflow from Emet Capital's position as a broker connecting eligible commercial borrowers with lenders. Importers should obtain customs-broker, tax and legal advice for classification, valuation, origin and duty liability rather than relying on finance content.

## At a Glance

| Issue | What the importer should do | Finance relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **Possible anti-dumping measure** | Check current official measures with a licensed customs broker | Do not size a facility from supplier price alone |
| **Duty amount or method uncertain** | Obtain a documented landed-cost estimate and downside range | Include contingency without presenting it as the legal assessment |
| **Supplier payment precedes import clearance** | Map deposit, balance, freight and clearance dates | Match draws and facility expiry to the whole cycle |
| **Goods are sold on customer terms** | Forecast inventory conversion and debtor collection | Test the period between duty payment and customer cash receipt |
| **Measure changes margin** | Reprice, renegotiate or reconsider the order | Debt cannot repair a structurally unprofitable shipment |

## Who This Is For

This guide is for Australian companies and commercial importers purchasing goods for resale, manufacturing or business use where an anti-dumping or countervailing measure may affect landed cost.

It excludes personal imports, consumer borrowing and legal or customs advice. A lender assesses a finance application; it does not replace Australian Border Force, the Anti-Dumping Commission, a customs broker or the importer's professional advisers.

## First Verify the Measure, Then Build the Funding Request

The Anti-Dumping Commission publishes current measures through the Dumping Commodity Register. An importer should check the register and relevant notices, then work with a licensed customs broker to apply the requirements to the actual shipment.

A useful verification pack records:

- the commercial product description and technical specification;
- tariff classification used for the import declaration;
- country of origin and country of export;
- supplier and exporter identity;
- purchase order, invoice and Incoterms;
- shipment or export date evidence;
- applicable measure, notice and variable factors; and
- the customs broker's written landed-cost estimate.

Do not assume that a previous shipment, a competitor's treatment or a supplier's statement proves the current outcome. Measures and applicable variables can change, and similar-looking goods can have different characteristics.

**Trade finance should be sized from a verified landed-cost workflow, not from the supplier invoice alone.**

## Use a Landed-Cost Funding Worksheet

A simple worksheet makes the timing gap visible. The figures below are illustrative only and are not a duty calculation.

| Stage | Illustrative amount | Payment timing | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Supplier deposit | $60,000 | Order confirmation | Purchase order and supplier invoice |
| Supplier balance | $140,000 | Before shipment | Invoice and inspection documents |
| Freight and insurance | $18,000 | Before arrival | Freight quote and forwarder invoice |
| Customs duty and import taxes | $22,000 | Clearance | Customs-broker estimate |
| Possible anti-dumping/countervailing duty | $48,000 | Clearance or as advised | Current measure and broker estimate |
| Port, clearance and delivery | $12,000 | Arrival and release | Forwarder and logistics quotes |
| **Illustrative landed cash requirement** | **$300,000** | Across the trade cycle | Consolidated worksheet |

If the importer had budgeted only $252,000, the possible additional duty creates a $48,000 gap before the goods can enter the sales cycle. But funding that gap only makes sense if the revised selling price, margin and customer demand still support the transaction.

The worksheet should therefore continue beyond customs:

1. expected warehouse receipt date;
2. quality checks or manufacturing inputs;
3. customer dispatch schedule;
4. invoice issue dates;
5. customer payment terms and realistic collection dates; and
6. the proposed facility repayment date.

## How the Duty Changes a Trade-Finance Assessment

### Facility Size

The request may need to cover more than supplier payment. If the structure funds only the overseas invoice but leaves the borrower unable to pay freight, import charges or duty, the inventory may not be released and cannot generate the expected sale proceeds.

### Borrower Contribution

A lender may expect the importer to contribute equity or fund some cost categories. The borrower should show what has already been paid, what remains available and whether contingency funds are genuinely accessible.

### Gross Margin and Debt Capacity

An additional duty can compress or eliminate margin. The importer should model base, downside and repriced cases. If customers will not accept a revised price and the shipment becomes loss-making, extra debt can magnify the loss rather than solve it.

### Security and Control

Depending on the facility, a lender may consider inventory, receivables, general security, property or another acceptable security package. Inventory location, title, insurance, customer concentration and the ability to monitor proceeds may affect structure. The [invoice finance guide](/resources/guides/invoice-finance-australia-complete-guide) explains the later receivables stage.

### Term and Exit

The term must cover the full cycle from supplier payment through clearance, sale, invoice and customer collection, with a sensible buffer. “Repay when the container lands” is not an exit if duty remains unpaid or customers buy on 60-day terms.

## Comparing Funding Structures

| Structure | Potential fit | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **Trade finance** | Supplier and import-stage payments linked to an identified trade cycle | Structure and eligible costs vary; duty coverage must be confirmed |
| **Purchase order finance** | A firm customer order supports supplier-stage production or purchase | The order, supplier and transaction must fit; it may not cover every landed cost |
| **Business line of credit** | Repeated timing gaps with variable draw needs | Limit, renewal and repayment discipline matter |
| **Working capital term loan** | A defined one-off landed-cost shortfall | Full upfront drawing may not match staged payments |
| **Invoice finance** | Eligible customer invoices exist after goods are delivered | Usually does not solve the earliest supplier or customs stage by itself |
| **Property-secured business finance** | A larger or longer gap has acceptable security and a clear commercial exit | Property is exposed if the repayment plan fails |

A combined facility stack can sometimes match different stages, but complexity creates its own risk. Priority, control of proceeds and total obligations must be clear. ## Evidence a Lender May Request

A finance-ready importer file may include:

- company and ownership details;
- recent bank statements, BAS, financial statements and management accounts;
- supplier history, purchase orders and pro forma invoices;
- customer orders, contracts or sales history;
- shipping, freight and insurance documents;
- the customs broker's classification and landed-cost estimate;
- evidence of applicable current measures and shipment dates;
- inventory reports and existing security registrations;
- the borrower's contribution and contingency position;
- a cash-flow forecast covering the entire conversion cycle; and
- a repayment plan tested against delayed clearance and customer payment.

This pack helps a lender understand the commercial transaction without asking the lender to give customs advice. The [business loan terms guide](/resources/guides/business-loan-terms-explained-australia) can help importers compare controls, events of default and repayment mechanics.

## When To Use Trade Finance

Trade finance may be worth assessing when:

- the goods, supplier, customers and payment sequence are identifiable;
- the importer has verified the possible measure and landed-cost range;
- the revised transaction remains commercially viable;
- funding can be matched to evidence and real payment dates;
- the business has a credible repayment path from sales or another source; and
- the borrower understands security, control and downside obligations.

## When Not To Use It

It may not be suitable when:

- classification or measure applicability is being guessed;
- the shipment is unprofitable once the possible duty is included;
- repayment depends on an unconfirmed customer or unrealistic sales timing;
- the requested term ends before inventory can convert to collected cash;
- the business is using new debt to conceal recurring losses; or
- the importer expects finance to replace customs, tax or legal advice.

Where a duty surprise has already created broader distress, the importer may need a full operational and creditor plan rather than one more shipment facility. The [business turnaround finance guide](/resources/guides/business-turnaround-finance-australia) explains that distinction.

## A Practical Importer Workflow

1. **Identify the goods precisely.** Capture specifications, tariff classification, origin, export country and exporter.
2. **Check official current measures.** Use the Dumping Commodity Register and relevant notices with a customs broker.
3. **Build three landed-cost cases.** Model expected, higher-cost and delayed-clearance outcomes.
4. **Test margin before borrowing.** Reprice or renegotiate where possible and confirm the order remains viable.
5. **Map every payment date.** Include supplier, freight, insurance, customs, duty, storage, delivery and local handling.
6. **Extend the map to customer cash.** Show inventory days, sales timing, invoice terms and collection assumptions.
7. **Request the evidenced gap only.** Match drawdowns and repayment to the real transaction.
8. **Keep a contingency plan.** Address duty variation, port delay, exchange-rate movement and slow customer payment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is anti-dumping duty in Australia?

Anti-dumping duty is an additional import duty associated with measures imposed on specified goods after Australia's trade-remedies process. Applicability depends on the current measure and shipment facts, so importers should check official records and obtain customs advice.

### What is a countervailing duty?

A countervailing duty is an additional duty connected with subsidised imports that have met the requirements for a countervailing measure. It is distinct from anti-dumping duty, although both are administered within Australia's trade-remedies system.

### Where can importers check current anti-dumping measures?

The Anti-Dumping Commission publishes current measures in the Dumping Commodity Register and related notices. Importers should verify the current entry and work with a licensed customs broker on their specific goods and shipment.

### Can trade finance pay anti-dumping duty?

Some business finance structures may include eligible landed costs, but coverage varies by lender and facility. The importer must disclose the duty, provide evidence and confirm permitted use of funds before relying on the facility.

### How does anti-dumping duty affect working capital?

The duty can increase cash required at import clearance before the goods are sold. This extends or enlarges the gap between supplier payment and customer receipts and can reduce gross margin.

### What documents support a finance request?

Useful documents include purchase orders, supplier invoices, shipping records, customs-broker estimates, current measure evidence, customer orders, financial statements, bank statements, inventory records and a complete cash-flow timeline.

### Does finance remove customs or pricing risk?

No. Finance supplies capital under agreed terms; it does not determine legal liability, prevent measure changes, guarantee clearance or make an unprofitable shipment viable.
