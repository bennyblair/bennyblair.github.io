Lenders assess hospitality property finance for a business borrower by looking at three parts together: the freehold or leasehold property, the trading business, and any liquor or occupancy licence that supports the going concern. A hotel, motel, pub, restaurant or venue file is usually judged on how those parts support security, serviceability and a documented repayment plan, not on a single bricks-and-mortar valuation in isolation.

This guide is general information on commercial, business-purpose hospitality property finance. It is not a rate sheet or an eligibility promise.

This page is for business-purpose borrowing only. The law provides the lowest level of protection to commercial loans, including loans to small businesses. Commercial loans are loans provided to companies and business owners. ## Understanding Hospitality Property Lending Challenges

Hospitality property finance is harder to fit into a standard commercial mortgage template because the asset, the operator and the licence often travel together.

### Why Traditional Banks Struggle With Hospitality

Bank property models often assume a tenant market and rent that another occupier could replace. A restaurant or café depends on location, offer, costs and the operator, which are harder to score than a leased shop with documented rent. Trading can move with seasons, competition and reputation. Lenders may decline, ask for more equity, or look only at the land and buildings. Specialised kitchens and theming can be expensive to install and worth much less to a different operator if the venue fails.

### Liquor Licence Valuation Complications

A liquor licence can be a large part of what a buyer pays for a hotel, pub or licensed restaurant, but it is not the same as land. Many lenders treat it as a regulatory permission that may support going-concern value, not as standard real-property security.

The licences and permits you need depend on your business type, business activities, state or territory and local government area, according to [business.gov.au](https://business.gov.au/registrations/register-licences-and-permits). Local councils and state or territory governments manage most licence and permits in this industry. You may need a licence or permit to sell alcohol to consume on site or take away. See the [accommodation and food services industry page](https://business.gov.au/planning/industry-information/accommodation-and-food-services-industry). This page does not decide which licence a venue needs, whether it can transfer, or what it is worth.

Licence type, conditions and transfer rules vary by jurisdiction. Some specialist hospitality lenders may give weight to a licence. Others exclude it and fund only the property. Regulatory action or a failed transfer can change the business even when the building is unchanged.

### Multi-Component Funding Requirements

A hospitality purchase often needs more than a property mortgage. The same settlement may include the freehold or lease, goodwill, a liquor licence, fit-out, equipment and working capital. Few lenders cover every piece on the same terms.

Buyers therefore compare a property facility with [business acquisition finance in Australia](/resources/guides/business-acquisition-finance-australia), equipment or fit-out finance, vendor terms and cash. Property settlement, business transfer, licence transfer and equipment handover may not run on the same clock. This page does not quote fees, deposits or a standard timetable.

## Structuring Hospitality Property Finance

Structure should match the components being bought and how the business will repay.

### Property Component Financing

The property element is still a commercial mortgage against land and buildings. For a hotel, motel or venue freehold, that is often arranged as [commercial property finance for hotel, motel and venue property arranged through a broker](/services/commercial-property-finance). How much of value a lender will consider depends on the asset, trading evidence, the borrower and the rest of the package. There is no published loan-to-value ratio that applies to every restaurant, hotel or pub.

Some specialist hospitality lenders look at trading history and may give limited weight to a licence. That can reduce cash the buyer must find elsewhere, and it can cost more than a loan against land only. Strata restaurant, café or hotel units can be harder because of body-corporate control and enforcement. The [commercial property loans Australia complete guide](/resources/guides/commercial-property-loans-australia-complete-guide) explains how hospitality sits beside other commercial assets.

### Business and Licence Funding

Goodwill and liquor licences usually sit outside a standard property mortgage. Options include vendor terms, a separate business facility, or [what is private lending](/resources/guides/what-is-private-lending-australia) sources that will look at a wider security package. Those pieces are often more expensive than the property mortgage because they are harder to enforce if trading fails.

Vendor finance is common where the seller will leave some of the business and licence price in. It can fill a gap a property lender will not fund. Repayment, subordination to the property mortgage and default terms need to be documented. A single private or specialist facility covering property, business and licence can be simpler to coordinate. Simpler is not the same as cheaper.

### Fit-Out and Equipment Finance

Kitchen plant, furniture, rooms and venue fit-out are often a large extra cost on a restaurant, hotel or motel. New, saleable equipment is usually easier to fund than custom joinery. Amounts and terms are set file by file.

## Lender Assessment of Hospitality Venues

Understanding what lenders read helps a buyer present a complete file.

### Trading History and Performance

Established venues with a clear trading record are easier to underwrite than a new concept with projections only. Documentation requirements vary between loans, but a cash flow statement, business plan, lease agreements and personal financial information are among the items [business.gov.au](https://business.gov.au/finance/funding/apply-for-a-business-loan) lists as paperwork that may be required. Seasonal venues need a serviceability story that matches the pattern. If a recent period was disrupted, show current trading rather than leaving the lender to guess.

### Location and Market Analysis

Location still matters, but "a good strip" is not evidence. Lenders look at precinct demand, whether the venue could be sold or re-let, and whether the local market can support the offer. A hotel or motel on a known tourism route is not assessed the same way as a suburban restaurant in a crowded category.

In a hypothetical example, two similar restaurants in the same suburb can still be treated differently if one has a longer trading record, a transferable licence and a fit-out another operator could use. This is an illustration, not a transaction, and it does not predict leverage, price or approval.

### Operational Expertise Assessment

Operator skill is part of the credit view. Previous venue ownership, hotel or motel management, or senior hospitality roles usually help more than a first hospitality purchase with no operating plan. Larger hotels and pubs also need a team story: who manages rooms or the kitchen, who holds the licence, and what happens if the key person leaves. A plan covering offer, roster and cash flow is more useful than a slogan.

## Specialist Hospitality Lenders vs Traditional Sources

Bank, non-bank and private lenders all appear on hospitality files. They are different risk appetites.

### Traditional Bank Lending

Major banks may fund hospitality property when trading is established, equity is strong and the operator is experienced. Conservative policy still screens out many viable venues. When the file fits, a bank can offer a longer term and a broader relationship. The trade-off is often a slower process and a security view that ignores licence and fit-out. Regional banks sometimes take a more local view of a hotel, motel or pub. That is a tendency, not a rule.

### Non-Bank Commercial Lenders

Non-bank commercial lenders are a common source for hospitality property because they are more used to trading-dependent security. Some will look at property, business and equipment together, and some will give limited weight to a licence. Pricing and conditions are usually heavier than bank property loans. Read each loan's terms.

### Private Lending Solutions

Private lenders sit at the more flexible end: limited trading history, credit issues, mixed hotel-plus-entertainment uses, or a settlement date a bank will not meet. [Private lending](/services/private-lending) is still property-secured commercial finance. It is not a consumer loan and it is not a promise of speed. Cost is usually higher and terms shorter. Exit planning matters: refinance once trading and documents are in order, sale, or another documented repayment event. ## Common Hospitality Finance Scenarios

Venue type changes the mix of property, licence, rooms and entertainment.

### Restaurant and Café Acquisitions

Restaurants and cafés are the most common hospitality property files, from a small suburban café to a large city freehold. Smaller files often lean on vendor terms for the business and licence, equipment finance for the kitchen, and equity for working capital. Leasehold removes the property mortgage. Freehold lets the land support a commercial mortgage, but it requires more capital at settlement.

### Hotel and Accommodation Finance

Hotel and motel property combines rooms, food and beverage, and often a liquor licence. Boutique hotels, motels and larger regional hotels are still assessed as property plus trading plus licence, but occupancy, room quality and capex weigh more than they do for a restaurant. Lenders may want a hospitality valuation that looks at trading potential, not only land sales. Strata hotel rooms under a management agreement are a narrower market. Many lenders decline individual rooms.

### Licensed Venue and Entertainment Financing

Pubs, hotels with a strong bar, nightclubs, live music rooms and event spaces are often treated as higher risk than a food-led restaurant. Revenue can be more volatile, and late-night trading can attract extra regulatory attention. An extended-hours licence can be valuable in a sale and still be excluded from security. Sound, lighting and staging are often funded with vendor terms, equity or a private facility that looks at the whole going concern.

## Managing Hospitality Lending Costs

Hospitality property finance usually costs more than a plain commercial mortgage because of trading risk, licence uncertainty and split structures. Cost is set file by file. This page does not quote interest rates or fees.

### Cost Factors

Price follows risk: property quality, location, trading evidence, operator experience, leverage, licence position and how many facilities are needed. Separating a cheaper property mortgage from dearer business funding can lower a blended cost on some files and add duplicate legal work on others. Compare the whole package: interest structure, fees, conditions, reports, personal guarantees and the repayment type. Ask for a fee list in writing before instructing valuation or legal work.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do lenders set leverage for hospitality property?

They look at the property, the trading business, any licence, the operator and the rest of the borrower's position, not at a single hospitality percentage. There is no published loan-to-value ratio that applies to every restaurant, hotel, motel or pub.

### How do lenders treat liquor licences on hotel, pub and restaurant files?

Many lenders exclude the licence from the security calculation even when it is a large part of the purchase price. Specialist hospitality lenders may give it some weight. Licence type, conditions and transfer rules are state-based. The licences and permits you need depend on your business type, business activities, state or territory and local government area.

### Can restaurant fit-outs and equipment be financed?

Often as a separate discussion from the property mortgage. Identifiable kitchen plant may suit equipment finance. Custom fit-out is harder. Emet Capital arranges commercial property-backed finance and does not promise an equipment-finance product. Amounts and terms are set by the equipment lender, not by this guide.

### What hospitality experience do lenders look for?

Previous venue ownership, hotel or motel management, or senior hospitality roles usually help. First-time operators can still present a file if the plan, equity and operating team are strong, but the security view is often more conservative.

### How long does hospitality property finance take?

There is no standard approval or settlement timetable. Timing depends on documents, valuation, legal work, licence transfer where a licence is part of the sale, and whether the lender is a bank, non-bank or private credit provider. A broker cannot promise a date.

### When is vendor finance used on hospitality purchases?

Often for goodwill and liquor licence components that a property lender will not fund. It can reduce cash at settlement if the seller will leave money in. It also creates a second security interest that usually needs to rank behind the property mortgage. This page does not quote vendor rates.

## Conclusion

Hospitality property finance is an assessment of freehold or leasehold property, trading and licence together. Hotels, motels, pubs, restaurants and venues fail standard templates when the lender cannot see how the building, the operator and the permissions support repayment.
